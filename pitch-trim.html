Adaptive Pitch Trim Explorer

Needs ArduPlane Stratos v2.1 or later. Not supported in v2.0 or lower: those versions do not have the PTCH_TRIM_SPD / PTCH_TRIM_OFS parameters.

A plane needs a different pitch attitude to fly level at each speed, but PTCH_TRIM_DEG is one number, right at one speed only. Adaptive pitch trim replaces it with a curve of three points (PTCH_TRIM_SPD1–3, PTCH_TRIM_OFS1–3). Enter or load a curve to see the trim it gives at every speed, or fit one from a learn-flight log exactly as the aircraft's own learner would.

Most people want this

Get a curve from a flight log

  1. Fly the legs. In FBWB or CRUISE, fly straight and level at 4–5 different speeds about 4–5 m/s apart, 5–10 s each, in calm air. Any firmware version works for this flight.
  2. Load the .bin in Fit a curve from a log below. You don't need to type anything first: the trim and AIRSPEED_MIN come from the log.
  3. Check the result. The blue dots on the chart should fall steadily as speed rises. A dot far off the others, or a "need 3 over 3m/s" message, means fly those legs again.
  4. Click "Use this fitted curve" to draw it, then "Copy as .param lines".
  5. Paste into Mission Planner (Config → Full Parameter List, or save as a .param file and load it). The lines are in the safe order, PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 last. The aircraft needs ArduPlane Stratos v2.1.
  6. Check in flight: hands-off FBWA at a few speeds should now hold height.
Optional

Look at or change a curve

  1. Load .param from your aircraft to see the curve it has now, or type values in Your curve.
  2. The chart shows the trim the aircraft will use at every airspeed; the list under the inputs flags problems (curve off, offsets not falling with speed, points inside the stall taper).
  3. Edit the numbers to try changes before writing them to the aircraft.

Rather learn the curve in flight with the RCx_OPTION 254 switch? See Learning in flight below; the aircraft then saves the curve itself and you only need this page to look at it.

    Fit a curve from a log

    Load the .bin from a learn flight (or any flight with steady level legs at several speeds). The file is read in your browser and never uploaded. The fit repeats the firmware learner step for step: 10 samples a second, kept only when roll is within ±5°, vertical speed under 0.5 m/s and the filtered airspeed changing by less than 0.5 m/s per second; 1 m/s speed bands that need 30 samples (3 s); then the same weighted least-squares fit, saved as three points.

    Why it matters
    
      
    Flying slow needs the nose higher, flying fast needs it lower. With one trim value the flight controller holds the wrong attitude as "level" at every speed but one:
    
      
      
        
    • FBWA, Course Hold, Auto Trim: hands-off, the plane climbs when you add throttle and sinks when you slow down, so you keep correcting with the pitch stick.
      • 
        
    • FBWB, CRUISE, AUTO: TECS carries a standing pitch correction to hold height and has to find it again after every speed change.
      • 
      
    
      
    The curve puts the right level attitude at every speed. It is off during takeoff, landing and VTOL. It does not measure angle of attack: it is pitch trim scheduled on airspeed.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    What you gain

    The simulated plane's level pitch changes only about 0.9° across its speed range; real airframes measured 2.5–3.7°, so expect larger effects. On four real logs the curve matched level pitch within 0.30–0.39°; stock KFF_THR2PTCH managed 0.42–1.38° at best, and only at −12 to −17, far outside its ±5 range.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Caveats
    
      
      
        
    • One weight and balance. Learn at your normal take-off weight; after a big payload, battery or CG change, learn again or recheck hands-off FBWA.
      • 
        
    • Basics first. Offsets are measured against PTCH_TRIM_DEG, so set that at cruise first, and tune TECS before learning.
      • 
        
    • Pitot and TECS modes only: nothing to gain, so it is optional there.
      • 
        
    • Gusty air: in SITL turbulence steady height hold was slightly looser with the curve (0.87 → 0.95 m RMS with a pitot, 0.97 → 1.06 m without; 8 and 9 of 12 runs). Learning is also slow in gusts.
      • 
        
    • No pitot: it runs on the wind estimate, and an error there shifts the whole curve along the speed axis.
      • 
        
    • No new stall protection. The taper only stops the curve raising the nose near AIRSPEED_MIN; FBWA still lets you slow into a stall as before.
      • 
        
    • KFF_THR2PTCH is ignored while the curve is active, since both do the same job.
      • 
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    How it works
    
      
    The offset is added to the pitch demand after the flight mode has computed it, in the same place PTCH_TRIM_DEG goes. It is pitch trim scheduled on airspeed; it does not measure or control angle of attack.
    
      
      
        
    • Off during takeoff, the landing (NAV_LAND) leg, VTOL and assisted flight. PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 = 0 turns it off completely.
      • 
        
    • Smooth: airspeed is filtered and the offset follows with about a 1 s time constant.
      • 
        
    • KFF_THR2PTCH is ignored while the curve is active, since it does the same job by throttle; it applies normally whenever the curve is off.
      • 
        
    • Valid curve: 0 < SPD1 < SPD2 < SPD3, offsets ±10°.
      • 
      
    
      
    Switch, RCx_OPTION = 254
    
      
    PositionEffect
    
        
    LOWOff (the saved curve is kept)
    
        
    MIDDLEOn
    
        
    HIGHLearn; moving off HIGH fits the curve and saves it
    
      
    
      
    With no switch assigned, the curve is used whenever one exists. The position at boot only chooses on or off; it never starts a learn.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Learning in flight
    
      
      
        
    1. Switch HIGH: Adaptive trim learn: fly level FBWB/CRUISE. A switch already HIGH at boot does not learn; move it off and back.
      2. 
        
    2. In FBWB or CRUISE fly straight and level at 4–5 speeds about 4–5 m/s apart, 5–10 s each. Progress every 10 s: Adaptive trim learn: N speeds.
      3. 
        
    3. Move the switch to MIDDLE. The curve is fitted, saved and printed, e.g. Adaptive trim: 14.5 +2.1 18.5 +1.0 22.5 +0.2. It needs at least 3 speeds over 3 m/s, otherwise nothing is saved.
      4. 
      
    
      
    Flying the legs
    
      
      
        
    • Pitch stick centred: in FBWB it commands a climb, and over 0.5 m/s drops the samples.
      • 
        
    • Roll within 5° for samples to count; turning around between legs is fine, samples for the same speed add up.
      • 
        
    • Throttle stick sets each speed (with a pitot); let it settle before it counts.
      • 
        
    • Calm air: gusts break the steadiness checks, so the counts climb slowly.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Setting it by hand or from a log
    
      
      
        
    1. Turn the curve off while measuring (switch LOW, or PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 0).
      2. 
        
    2. Fly level in FBWB or CRUISE at a slow, a middle and a fast speed, the slow one above 1.25 × AIRSPEED_MIN.
      3. 
        
    3. Offset at each speed = measured level pitch − PTCH_TRIM_DEG. From a log use ATT.Pitch (real pitch) with CTUN.As, not CTUN.Pitch, which already has the trim removed. Offsets should fall as speed rises.
      4. 
        
    4. Enter SPD2, SPD3 and the offsets first, PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 last, so a half-entered curve never goes live.
      5. 
        
    5. Check with hands-off FBWA at each speed: it should hold altitude.
      6. 
      
    
      
    The fit above does steps 2–3 for you from the .bin.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Pitot or no pitot
    
      
    SetupRecommendation
    
        
    Pitot, flown mostly in TECS modes (AUTO, CRUISE, FBWB)Optional. Steady flight is unchanged and speed changes are slightly worse (0.1–0.2 m).
    
        
    Pitot, flies FBWAUse it, PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 around AIRSPEED_CRUISE.
    
        
    No pitotUse it in all modes, PTCH_TRIM_SPD1 not below the trim-throttle speed.
    
      
    
      
    Without a pitot it runs on synthetic airspeed (GPS speed corrected by the wind estimate). A wind-estimate error shifts the curve along the speed axis, so learn on a calm day, fly a few circles first, and fly each speed upwind and downwind. Without a pitot TECS also sets throttle from pitch against trim, so the curve affects throttle in FBWB, CRUISE and AUTO as well.
    
      
    Tune TECS first (run a transmitter TECS helper with the switch LOW), then learn the curve.
